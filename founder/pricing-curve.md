# Price Sensitivity Analysis (Van Westendorp PSM)

Conducted on 20 simulated buyers representing multi-sport working professionals, hybrid runners/padel players, gym lifters, and student athletes.

| Metric | Price | What It Means |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Point of Marginal Cheapness (PMC)** | **$2.03 / mo** | Below this, too many buyers assume the app is low-quality shovelware or ad-infested. |
| **Optimal Price Point (OPP)** | **$2.04 / mo** | The point of minimum resistance where as many say "too cheap" as "too expensive". |
| **Indifference Price Point (IPP)** | **$4.04 / mo** | The point of equal bargain vs. expensive perception. Modern SaaS standard anchor. |
| **Point of Marginal Expensiveness (PME)** | **$7.99 / mo** | Above this, resistance rises sharply and churn accelerates for consumer fitness apps. |

---

### Acceptable Price Corridor
**$2.03 to $7.99 / month** (or **$24 to $59 / year**)

- **Current Pricing Tested:** $4.99/mo or $29.99/yr ($2.50/mo effective).
- **Positioning:** The current $4.99/mo sits comfortably inside the acceptable range, just above the Indifference Point ($4.04/mo), while the annual $29.99/yr pricing ($2.50/mo) sits right next to the Optimal Price Point ($2.04/mo).
- **Key Pricing Finding:** Buyers did not reject the price itself—they rejected paying *because the free tier and the 5-friend referral loop were so generous that nobody needed to pay*. If the paywall triggers are sharpened around deep coach analytics and cross-sport recovery balancing, pricing can safely increase to $6.99–$9.99/mo or $49.99/yr.
