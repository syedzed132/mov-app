# Product lens

Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **1-Click CSV import works backward from user pain:** The single best product decision in the update. Lifters remain locked into incumbent apps (Strong, Hevy) solely because abandoning years of personal record (PR) history feels intolerable. Engineering a zero-friction data migration bridge solves switching anxiety before the user even logs their first set, creating an immediate, high-trust "magic moment" in minute two of onboarding.
- **Multi-sport tracking matches the reality of the athlete:** Dedicated trainees rarely live exclusively in the squat rack; they play tennis, padel, run, and cycle. Acknowledging that human movement is multi-modal and connecting non-lifting energy expenditure (via duration and METs) back into dynamic nutritional guidance aligns the software with how modern athletes actually live.
- **Intelligent freemium guardrails protect the core habit:** Keeping core workout logging, CSV import, and data synchronization free guarantees that the daily mechanical utility remains uninhibited. Gating computationally expensive operations (unlimited AI meal vision/voice parsing) and advanced analytical intelligence (Adaptive AI Coach) behind Pro establishes a clean, honest exchange of value without cheapening the essential logging tool.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The Frankenstein Trap (Feature bloat destroying craft):**
   Instead of disciplining the product after initial feedback, the roadmap expanded exponentially: a full social network, an RPG progression system, a multi-sport activity tracker, a viral referral pyramid, and dynamic MET engines bolted onto an already unwieldy foundation (workout tracking, 1,300+ exercises, meal/macro scanning, and an AI coach). When an early-stage product attempts to be Strong, MyFitnessPal, Strava, and an RPG video game all at once, every sub-feature will feel shallow, unpolished, and buggy. Focus means saying no to 100 good ideas to execute one brilliantly; MOV is currently saying yes to everything.
2. **The "Ghost Town" social feed and transactional referral spam:**
   Social feeds only feel alive when there is dense, localized user activity. For the first several thousand users, opening the social tab will reveal an empty void—an uninspiring seam that damages the premium aura of the product. Furthermore, tying customer acquisition to an "Invite 5 friends for 1 free month" loop turns users into unwanted referral spammers. It degrades the brand from an aspirational athletic instrument into a growth-hacked scheme, polluting the network with disengaged, coerced accounts rather than genuine peers.
3. **Juvenile RPG gamification corroding athletic credibility:**
   Awarding artificial "Agility XP", "Strength XP", and video game titles like "Playmaker" or "Iron Vanguard" trivializes serious athletic training. Real lifters and competitive athletes care deeply about genuine physical progression: 1RM records, weekly tonnage, rate of perceived exertion (RPE), volume milestones, and body composition. Cartoonish gamification creates an identity crisis: it alienates serious trainees who view it as a childish gimmick, and it clashes directly with the sophisticated, scientific tone required for the paid "Adaptive AI Coach".

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. Can you describe what MOV is in one single sentence without using the word "and"?
2. What does a brand-new user experience in the social feed in their first 60 seconds when none of their real-world friends are on the platform?
3. Which single feature will you guarantee is 10x faster, cleaner, and more delightful than any existing app on the market? If you are simultaneously building for runners, lifters, padel players, and gamers, how can any of them be best-in-class?
4. Who is the exact person you designed the RPG titles ("Iron Vanguard") for? Have you tested whether an experienced lifter or competitive runner feels pride or embarrassment when that badge appears on their screen?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Excise the RPG gamification completely:** Strip out attribute XP, fantasy titles, and agility/strength levels. Replace them with authentic athletic craft: elegant, high-taste visualizations of real personal records, volume curves, pace trends, and biometric milestones.
2. **Shelve the public social feed and referral bounty:** Hide the social feed until single-player 30-day retention reaches at least 45%. Remove the 5-friend referral gate for Pro; elite products spread through unsolicited word-of-mouth earned by flawless utility, not through transactional friend-bribing.
3. **Keep multi-sport strictly subservient to systemic recovery and nutrition:** Do not build a second-rate Strava clone with sport-specific communities. Restrict multi-sport input to simple, beautiful activity logging (sport, duration, MET/calorie impact) whose sole purpose is keeping the daily energy and recovery dashboard accurate.
4. **Make the CSV import the centerpiece of onboarding:** Perfect the import experience so that uploading an export from Strong or Hevy renders a stunning, instant visual recap of the lifter's entire training career in under five seconds. Win the customer at the moment of migration with undeniable craft.
