# Offers lens

Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Dramatic reduction of Switching Effort & Sacrifice via 1-Click CSV Import:** In the value equation, effort and sacrifice are the primary points where conversion dies. Competitors like Strong and Hevy hold users captive because abandoning years of logged PRs and workout history is painful. The 1-click CSV import immediately neutralizes this friction, porting historical equity and delivering instant time-to-value on Day 1.
- **Initial guardrail on costly AI inference:** Capping free meal tracking at 5 AI scans per week is a crucial step toward protecting unit economics. It transforms what was previously an uncapped variable API cost into a teaser that demonstrates the speed of AI capture before requiring an upgrade for heavy daily use.
- **Multi-sport MET tracking broadens the dream outcome for hybrid athletes:** Modern fitness consumers rarely just lift; they play padel, tennis, run, and play soccer. Traditional lifting trackers leave a massive gap in daily energy balance by ignoring court and field sports. Calculating expenditure across multiple disciplines helps the buyer get closer to their overarching dream outcome (total body composition and athletic performance) without juggling disjointed apps.
- **RPG attribute progression (XP and dynamic titles) reduces perceived time delay:** Physical body recomposition takes months or years of grinding. Awarding attribute XP (Agility, Strength, Endurance) and dynamic titles ('Playmaker', 'Iron Vanguard') provides immediate micro-rewards and instant feedback loops after each session, shortening the psychological time delay between effort and gratification.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Broken referral offer economics (high social friction for a trivial reward, with cannibalization risk):**
   Asking a user to recruit *5 friends* in exchange for one free month of a $4.99 ($2.50/mo on annual) subscription is an asymmetric, low-value offer. Social capital is precious; consumers do not burn their relationships or spam five friends to save less than five dollars. Furthermore, if power users do successfully refer cohorts, they earn continuous free months, allowing your highest-frequency, highest-cost users to consume unlimited LLM inference and cloud storage without ever paying a dollar.
2. **Dilution of the core dream outcome into an unfocused identity crisis:**
   A Grand Slam Offer requires a starving market with a specific, urgent pain. By combining hardcore hypertrophy tracking (RPE, set-by-set overload), casual multi-sport calorie estimations (padel, soccer), a Strava-style social network, and RPG gaming stats, MOV tries to serve everyone and ends up speaking to no one. Dedicated lifters looking to maximize muscle mass will view RPG titles and casual soccer tracking as distracting gimmicks, while casual recreational athletes will find strength progression and macro tracking intimidating and bloated.
3. **Competing as a low-margin commodity with zero risk-reversal or offer stacking:**
   At $4.99/month and $29.99/year, MOV is competing on price in a hyper-competitive commodity app market. After platform store cuts (15–30%) and variable API costs for unlimited voice parsing, meal vision, and the Adaptive AI Coach, net margins remain thin. Selling software features alone without an offer stack (e.g., periodization templates, nutrition blueprints, plateau-breaking protocols) or a clear risk-reversal guarantee leaves the perceived likelihood of success low and churn high.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. **Referral economics:** Why would an active user expend the substantial social friction required to convince 5 friends to download an app just to save $4.99, and if they do, what prevents your heaviest users from cycling referrals to never pay for Pro?
2. **The target buyer and dream outcome:** Between the competitive lifter obsessed with progressive overload and exact macros, the recreational weekend padel player, and the gamified fitness consumer, who is the primary customer writing you the check, and what specific dream transformation are they buying?
3. **Paywall conversion urgency:** If a user receives complete core workout logging, full social feed access, CSV import, cloud sync, and 5 free AI scans every week for free, what acute, unavoidable pain forces them to upgrade to MOV Pro instead of simply pacing their free weekly scans?
4. **Offer stack and pricing power:** What high-value, low-cost assets could you bundle with the app (e.g., 12-week body transformation blueprints, periodized training blocks, automated weekly progress reviews) to charge $12.99/month or $79.99/year and make the offer an absolute no-brainer?
5. **Perceived likelihood and accuracy:** Multi-sport calorie expenditure based on standard MET formulas is notoriously noisy. How do you convince an athlete pursuing precise body composition goals that your dynamic calorie calculations are accurate enough to guide their nutritional intake rather than sabotage their progress?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Redesign the viral loop into an aligned two-sided offer:**
   Replace the "Invite 5 friends for 1 free month" mechanic with a frictionless, high-value two-sided invitation: give subscribers the ability to grant 1 free month of VIP Pro to a workout partner or teammate. If the referred friend completes their first week of tracked activity, the referrer receives 1 month of Pro or an exclusive training module bonus, with lifetime referral credits capped (e.g., maximum 3 free months) so paying power users cannot permanently evade monetization.
2. **Tighten the freemium boundary to manufacture genuine upgrade urgency:**
   Do not give away permanent high-utility features for free. Limit historical trend data on the free tier to a rolling 30–45 days, restrict multi-sport calorie aggregation to Pro, and keep the deepest RPG progression behind the paywall. The free tier must act as a compelling preview that creates hunger for the full system, rather than a permanent home.
3. **Stack the offer and eliminate commodity low pricing:**
   Raise MOV Pro pricing to at least $9.99–$14.99/month or $59.99–$79.99/year. Transform the product from a cheap utility into a complete outcome bundle by stacking it with high-perceived-value, zero-marginal-cost digital assets—such as structured sport-specific strength programs, targeted macro calibration guides, and weekly automated coaching audits.
4. **Implement a performance risk-reversal guarantee:**
   Differentiate from generic app store subscriptions by offering a 30-day or 60-day performance guarantee for annual members: if the user follows the Adaptive AI Coach's progressive overload and nutrition targets for 60 days and fails to hit a documented strength milestone or body composition target, offer a full refund. This elevates perceived likelihood of achievement and eliminates buyer hesitation.
